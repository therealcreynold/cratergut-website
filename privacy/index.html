Cratergut privacy policy

What Cratergut collects

Cratergut itself asks for nothing. There is no Cratergut account to create and no way to type anything that is sent anywhere. Everything except online play works without signing in to anything at all.

Online play is the one exception, and it is worth being exact about. It uses Apple's Game Center, so iOS may ask you to sign in with the Apple Account your device already has. Cratergut never sees your Apple Account, your email address or your password. The only thing that crosses the network is the opaque player identifier Apple hands the game, plus the moves of the match itself. There is no chat, and no name, roster or free text is ever sent.

Advertising is what keeps it free, and the advertising networks do collect things:

  • A device identifier, used to count how often an advert has been shown and to detect fraud. It is not used to choose adverts based on your interests: Cratergut asks Google for non-personalised adverts on every request, everywhere in the world. There is no setting for that and no prompt, because there is no other behaviour.
  • Approximate location, worked out from your internet address, at roughly the level of a city or region. Not your precise location.
  • How you interact with adverts: that one was shown, and whether it was tapped.
  • Diagnostics, such as a record of a crash, to work out what went wrong.

Who collects it

One company: Google, through AdMob. There is no mediation layer and no other advertising network in the game, so Google's privacy policy is the only other one that governs anything here. If that ever changes, this page changes with it before the change ships.

Choosing

Cratergut does not track you, and never asks to. Apple requires an app to ask permission before tracking, and you will never see that request from this game, because there is nothing to ask for — which also means Cratergut will not appear in the iOS Settings app under Privacy & Security → Tracking. That list only holds apps that asked.

In Europe and the United Kingdom you will see a privacy form before any advert; in parts of the United States you will see a notice about the sale and sharing of personal information. Elsewhere there is no prompt at all. Wherever the rules that apply to you give a standing right to change your mind, the game shows Settings → Privacy Options, and that is where you do it.

Declining costs you nothing. The game is identical either way.

Buying Remove Ads stops the adverts that interrupt you. It does not end the subject entirely: the optional adverts you choose to watch stay available on purpose, and each of those is still a request to Google.

Online play

Playing against someone else uses Apple's Game Center. Apple handles finding your opponent and carrying messages between you; what passes between the two devices is which buttons were pressed, and nothing else. Your opponent sees the display name your Game Center account already shows to other players, and nothing you type in the game is ever sent to them.

Children

Cratergut is not directed at children under 13 and is not in the Kids Category. It does not knowingly collect information from children under 13.

Keeping and deleting

Nothing is stored on our own servers, because there are none. Your progress lives on your device and is removed when you delete the game. What the advertising networks retain is governed by their own policies, and the links above explain how to ask them about it.
